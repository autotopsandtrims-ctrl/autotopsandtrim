How to fix a sagging headliner (and when the quick fix will not hold)

Pins, adhesive and steam all have their place — and all of them fail on a headliner whose foam has broken down. Here is how to tell which one you have.

A sagging headliner is one of the most common jobs that walks into an upholstery shop, and it is also one of the most commonly mis-diagnosed. Almost everyone assumes the glue has failed. Usually it has not.

What is actually happening above your head

A headliner is not one layer. It is a rigid backing board, a thin layer of foam bonded to it, and the fabric bonded to the foam. The fabric is not glued to the board at all — it is glued to the foam.

That foam is polyurethane, and heat and time break it down. Once it crumbles, nothing is holding the fabric up, because the thing it was attached to has turned to orange-brown dust. If you press a sagging headliner and your fingers come away with a fine powder, that is the foam, and it is the whole story.

This is why headliners fail in the middle of the roof first, and why they fail on cars that live outside in the Carolina sun far sooner than on garaged ones.

The DIY fixes, honestly rated

FixWhat it doesHonest verdict
Twist pinsPins the fabric back to the board mechanicallyGenuinely works as a holding measure, and it is reversible. It looks like what it is, and it does not stop the foam breaking down further.
Spray adhesive through the fabricAttempts to re-bond fabric to board in placeAlmost always disappointing. There is still dead foam between the two surfaces, so you are gluing fabric to powder. It also stains the face of the fabric.
Steam and rollerSoftens and re-lays the fabricCan buy time on a liner that is only just starting to lift at an edge. Useless once the foam is gone.
Removing the board and recovering itOld fabric and all remaining foam scraped off, new foam-backed material bonded to the bare boardThe only fix that actually lasts, because it replaces the layer that failed.

The test that tells you which one you need

Push up gently on the sagging area with a flat palm.

  • If it springs back and stays for a while, the bond has released at an edge and the foam is probably still intact. A careful re-lay may hold.
  • If it feels soft and grainy, or your hand comes away dusty, the foam has disintegrated. No adhesive will fix that — there is nothing left to bond to.
  • If the fabric has separated across most of the roof, the decision is already made for you.

Why we recover the board rather than glue the fabric back

When we recover a headliner, the board comes out of the car, every trace of the old foam is scraped back to clean board, and new foam-backed headliner material is bonded to it. That is more work than spraying adhesive through the old fabric, and it is the reason the repair does not come back in a year.

It is also the moment to deal with anything else the board has been hiding — sunroof shade tracks, wiring for interior lights, and trim clips that have gone brittle.
